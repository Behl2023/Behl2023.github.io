Understanding Double-Digest RAD Sequencing

How a reproducible fraction of the genome can be sampled across many individuals for SNP-based research.

What is ddRAD?

Double-digest restriction-site associated DNA sequencing is a reduced-representation method. Instead of sequencing the entire genome, it targets fragments generated consistently by two restriction enzymes.

Why use ddRAD?

It enables population-scale genotyping at lower cost than whole-genome sequencing and is useful for population structure, connectivity, genetic diversity, and conservation research.

Strengths

  • Scales to many individuals
  • Generates thousands of genome-wide markers
  • Works with or without a high-quality reference genome
  • Useful for non-model organisms

Limitations and technical bias

  • Allele dropout at mutated restriction sites
  • Missing data from uneven coverage
  • Batch effects from library, platform, or read-length differences
  • Sensitivity to size-selection consistency
  • Results depend strongly on filtering choices

Design questions

Researchers should consider sample size, enzyme choice, expected genome complexity, read length, sequencing depth, plate design, reference availability, and how batches will be balanced.
