A Practical Introduction to Population Genetics

Core concepts for interpreting genetic variation within and among populations.

Allele and genotype

An allele is an alternative form of a genetic variant. A genotype describes the alleles carried by an individual at a locus.

Heterozygosity

A measure of genetic variation based on the frequency of individuals carrying different alleles at a locus.

Gene flow and drift

Gene flow moves alleles among populations, while drift changes frequencies through random sampling, especially in small populations.

Population structure

Non-random distribution of genetic variation across geographic, ecological, or demographic groups.

FST

A measure of genetic differentiation among populations. Interpretation depends on the organism, marker set, sampling design, and uncertainty.

PCA

An unsupervised method that summarises major axes of variation without assigning individuals to predefined ancestry groups.

ADMIXTURE

A model-based approach that estimates ancestry proportions under assumptions about the number of genetic clusters.

DAPC

A discriminant method that maximises separation among predefined or inferred groups after dimension reduction.

Effective population size

The size of an idealised population experiencing the same amount of genetic drift as the observed population.

Panmixia

A condition of approximately random mating with little detectable population subdivision, within the limits of the data and methods used.

Interpretation warning: No single method should determine the biological conclusion. Population structure should be evaluated using complementary analyses, quality-control checks, sampling design, biological knowledge, and uncertainty.
