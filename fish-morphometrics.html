Practical field guide

How to collect morphometric data from a fish

A repeatable, species-independent workflow for accurate body measurements with reduced observer error, specimen distortion and data-entry mistakes.

1. Prepare the equipment

Essential tools

  • Fish measuring board or ichthyometer
  • Digital Vernier caliper
  • Electronic balance
  • Waterproof labels and permanent marker
  • Camera and visible scale bar
  • Gloves and cleaning materials

Standardise first

  • Use the same units, instruments and landmark definitions for all fish.
  • Check the balance calibration and caliper zero before measuring.
  • Record whether specimens are fresh, frozen or preserved.
  • Assign a unique specimen ID before collecting any measurement.

2. Position the fish correctly

  1. Place the fish on its left side on a flat, horizontal surface.
  2. Keep the body straight without stretching or compressing it.
  3. Close the mouth gently.
  4. Place the snout against the fixed stop of the measuring board.
  5. Arrange the caudal fin according to the chosen length definition.
  6. Remove excess surface water consistently before weighing.

Measure straight anatomical distances. Do not follow the curved body surface unless the protocol specifically requires a curved measurement.

3. Core morphometric measurements

Total length (TL)

Measure from the most anterior point of the snout to the longest point of the caudal fin.

Fork length (FL)

Measure from the snout tip to the deepest point of the caudal-fin fork.

Standard length (SL)

Measure from the snout tip to the posterior end of the hypural plate. Exclude the caudal fin.

Body mass

Blot excess water consistently and record the value after the balance reading stabilises.

Head length (HL)

Measure from the anterior tip of the snout to the posterior edge of the operculum.

Maximum body depth (BD)

Measure the greatest vertical distance between dorsal and ventral body margins.

4. Additional measurements

Cranial traits

  • Snout length
  • Eye diameter
  • Interorbital width
  • Upper-jaw length

Body and fin traits

  • Pre-dorsal length
  • Pre-pectoral length
  • Pre-pelvic length
  • Pre-anal length
  • Caudal-peduncle length and depth
  • Pectoral-, pelvic-, dorsal- and anal-fin lengths

The landmark definition for every variable must be documented because identical measurement names may be defined differently among studies.

5. Recommended measurement order

  1. Verify specimen ID, species and collection metadata.
  2. Photograph the fish with a visible scale bar and ID label.
  3. Record body mass.
  4. Measure TL, FL and SL.
  5. Measure head and body dimensions.
  6. Measure fins and smaller cranial traits.
  7. Record sex, maturity stage, abnormalities and damage.
  8. Repeat selected measurements for quality control.

6. Quality control

  • Repeat at least 10% of specimens without viewing the first measurement.
  • Cross-calibrate observers using the same subset of fish.
  • Check that TL is normally greater than or equal to FL, and FL is greater than SL.
  • Flag damaged fins, curved bodies and uncertain landmarks.
  • Do not silently overwrite unusual values; verify and document them.

7. Suggested raw-data columns

Specimen_ID · Species · Site · Date · Observer · Condition · Sex · Maturity · Mass_g · TL_mm · FL_mm · SL_mm · HL_mm · BD_mm · Snout_mm · Eye_mm · Jaw_mm · Predorsal_mm · Prepectoral_mm · Prepelvic_mm · Preanal_mm · CaudalPeduncleDepth_mm · Notes

Keep one row per fish and one variable per column. Avoid merged cells, mixed units and manual formatting as data.
